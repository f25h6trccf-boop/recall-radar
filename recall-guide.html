Vehicle Safety Recalls: What They Mean and What to Do

A vehicle safety recall is issued when a manufacturer or NHTSA identifies a safety-related defect or a failure to meet an applicable motor vehicle safety standard. Recalls can affect vehicles, equipment, tires, or child restraints.

Recall campaign versus open recall

A recall campaign may cover a range of models or production dates. Seeing a campaign associated with your vehicle's model does not mean your individual VIN is included or that a repair is still outstanding. A VIN-specific check is needed to determine open recall status.

What should I do if I see a campaign?

  1. Check your full VIN with NHTSA's official recall lookup.
  2. Read the manufacturer's recall notice and any safety instructions.
  3. Contact an authorized dealer or the manufacturer to confirm eligibility, parts availability, and scheduling.
  4. Follow any urgent safety guidance, including stop-driving or park-outside instructions if applicable.
  5. Keep documentation of completed recall repairs.

Do recall repairs cost money?

Safety recall remedies are generally provided free by the manufacturer, subject to applicable legal and program conditions. Contact the manufacturer or NHTSA if you have questions about your particular vehicle or a charge.

How does Recall Radar help?

Recall Radar displays public model-level campaign data and lets you save vehicles locally to compare campaign listings when you return. It is not an official VIN-specific open-recall status service, and it cannot send background notifications while your browser is closed.
