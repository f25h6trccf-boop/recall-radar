How Recall Radar works

  1. Enter a 17-character VIN to identify the vehicle.
  2. Review NHTSA model-level recall campaign records.
  3. Save vehicles in My Garage to compare campaigns when you return.
  4. Confirm VIN-specific open recall status through the official NHTSA recall checker.

Recall Radar does not determine whether an individual VIN has an unrepaired safety recall or provide background alerts.

Frequently asked questions about vehicle recalls

Does a VIN decoder tell me whether my car has an open recall?

No. A VIN decoder identifies vehicle characteristics. Recall Radar then displays recall campaigns associated with the decoded year, make and model. Only a VIN-specific recall status check from NHTSA or the manufacturer can help determine whether a recall applies to your individual vehicle and remains unrepaired.

Why might Recall Radar show a campaign that does not apply to my car?

A recall campaign can cover only certain production dates, equipment configurations or VIN ranges within a model. A year, make and model match alone is not enough to establish eligibility.

What if no campaigns are returned?

An empty result is not proof that a vehicle is recall-free. Public data may be unavailable, delayed or not matched to the decoded model name. Always verify through the official NHTSA VIN recall checker.

How often does My Garage check for changes?

Saved vehicles are checked when you return to this website in the same browser, or when you choose to check them. Recall Radar does not send push notifications, run checks while the browser is closed, or determine VIN-specific repair status.

Where can I learn what to do about a safety recall?

Read our vehicle safety recall guide for steps to confirm a campaign, contact a dealer and follow urgent manufacturer safety instructions. Our VIN guide explains how to find your vehicle identification number.
