What Is a VIN? How to Find and Decode Your Vehicle Identification Number

A vehicle identification number (VIN) is a unique identifier assigned to a vehicle. Most modern road vehicles use a 17-character VIN. VINs use letters and numbers, excluding I, O, and Q to avoid confusion with other characters.

Where can I find my VIN?

  • Look through the windshield at the lower driver-side dashboard.
  • Check the certification label on the driver-side door jamb.
  • Look at your registration, title, or insurance documents.

What does a VIN tell you?

VIN decoding can help identify the manufacturer, model year, vehicle line, and other characteristics. Available detail varies by vehicle and manufacturer. A decoded VIN does not prove a vehicle's mechanical condition, ownership history, or whether a particular safety recall repair is outstanding.

How can I check recalls?

Use Recall Radar to decode your VIN and explore model-level recall campaigns. For a determination of whether your specific vehicle has an unrepaired safety recall, use the official NHTSA VIN lookup or ask the manufacturer or dealer.

Is it safe to share a VIN?

A VIN is commonly visible on vehicles, but it can still be linked to a particular car. Avoid posting it unnecessarily. Recall Radar saves garage VINs in the browser you use; see our Privacy Policy.
